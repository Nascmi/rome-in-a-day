# Design Guardrails

These rules protect the identity of Rome Wasn't Built in a Day. They outrank roadmap items, content ideas, monetization opportunities, and requests to increase playtime.

## The Immutable Rules

1. If a feature does not reinforce “build the impossible in one day,” do not build it.
2. The clock is the principal antagonist.
3. Every campaign must tell a complete story between dawn and sunset.
4. Every reset must feel like progress.
5. The player should always understand why an attempt failed.
6. Visible city growth is more important than larger numbers.
7. Planning should become more important than tapping over time.
8. Permanent progress should create new strategies, not merely larger multipliers.
9. New complexity should replace or deepen old complexity, not stack thoughtlessly on top.
10. Later chapters must deepen the original premise, never replace it with a generic empire simulator.
11. The final fifteen seconds should matter.
12. Never add a mechanic solely to increase playtime.
13. Never punish a player for returning tomorrow.
14. Monetization must never weaken the meaning of effort, failure, or eventual victory.

## Feature Test

Before adding a system, answer:

- How does this make the one-day attempt more dramatic?
- What new decision does the player make?
- What existing decision becomes less important or is removed?
- How will the result be visible in the city?
- How does failure remain understandable and productive?
- Does this preserve a calm, legible interface?

If those answers are weak, the feature is not ready.

## Emotional Shape of a Day

Every attempt should have four recognizable acts:

### Morning — Confidence

The player selects a plan, assigns the first crews, and establishes an opening.

### Afternoon — Adaptation

The initial plan meets resource pressure and a midday event. The player adjusts.

### Evening — Commitment

Incomplete goals become urgent. The player chooses what must be finished and what can be abandoned.

### Final Light — Consequence

The clock becomes unmistakable. A final order creates one last strategic commitment. Victory or failure should feel earned rather than arbitrary.

## Empire Rule

The Empire exists because Rome exists.

Every expansion must still be expressible as:

> Accomplish something historically enormous before sunset. Fail, learn, and return stronger tomorrow.

If the game can no longer be explained that way, it has drifted away from its identity.
